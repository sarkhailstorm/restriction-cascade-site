Why Confluence edit restrictions don't inherit (and how to fix it)

In Confluence Cloud, child pages inherit view restrictions from their parent, but not edit restrictions. Here is what that means, the workarounds that exist, and how to make editors follow the page tree automatically.

Updated 23 November 2026 · 5 minute read

The short answer

  • View restrictions are inherited. If only some people can view a page, every page below it is hidden from everyone else too.
  • Edit restrictions are not. They apply only to the page where you set them. The pages below it can be edited by anyone who can view them and is allowed to edit in the space.
  • Atlassian has tracked the request to change this as CONFCLOUD-5095 since 2006, and it is still open.

What this looks like in practice

Say the finance team restricts editing on Finance Policies so only finance editors can change it. A colleague then adds Budget 2027 under it. The new page starts with no edit restrictions of its own, so anyone in the space who can edit pages can change it, and the same goes for every page added below it later.

Pages moved or copied into the tree keep whatever edit restrictions they already had, which is often none.

Your options without an app

  1. Restrict each page by hand. Open the page, choose Share → Specific access, and add the people and groups who may edit. It works, but it has to be repeated for every existing page and every page added, moved or copied in later, and it is easy to miss one.
  2. Tighten the space permissions. Limit who can add and edit pages in the whole space. This protects the tree, but it also locks every other page in the space.
  3. Move the content to its own space. A separate space with strict permissions keeps sensitive pages apart, at the cost of splitting related content across spaces.

None of these keep up on their own. As soon as someone adds a page under the restricted one, it is open again until somebody notices.

Make edit restrictions follow the page tree

Restriction Cascade is our Confluence Cloud app for exactly this. You make a page or folder a cascade root, and its editors are applied to everything below it, including pages added later.

  1. Restrict editing on the parent page: Share → Specific access, then add the people and groups who may edit.
  2. Open the page's ••• menu and choose Apps → Restriction Cascade.
  3. Choose Merge or Mirror.
  4. Click Save and apply to all pages below. You can preview the changes first.

From then on, pages created, moved, copied or restored below the root get the same editors within about a minute, and when the root's editors change, the pages below follow.

Merge

Pages anyone could edit get the root's editors. Pages that already restrict editing keep their editors and gain the root's.

Mirror

Every page gets exactly the root's editors. Anyone else listed as an editor is changed to view-only.

Exclude

Shields a branch from a cascade set higher up. Roots can be nested, and the nearest one decides.

Questions

Do view restrictions inherit in Confluence Cloud?

Yes. If a page can only be viewed by some people, the pages below it are hidden from everyone else as well.

Can I make edit restrictions inherit without an app?

Not automatically. You can restrict editing page by page, or limit who can edit in the whole space, but neither follows the page tree as it changes.

Will Restriction Cascade change who can see my pages?

No. It only grants or removes edit access, and it only gives edit access to people who can already view the page.
